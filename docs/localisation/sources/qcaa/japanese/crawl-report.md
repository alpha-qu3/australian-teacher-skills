## Verified Curriculum Structure (minimum extraction)

- **Subject family / course type:** Languages / General senior syllabus.
- **Implementation statement:** "For implementation with students who will complete their study of the course in 2026 or beyond" (official page, syllabus navigation section).
- **Syllabus objectives (6):** Comprehend Japanese to understand information, ideas, opinions and experiences; Identify tone, purpose, context and audience to infer meaning; Analyse and evaluate information and ideas to draw conclusions; Apply knowledge of language elements of Japanese to construct meaning; Structure, sequence and synthesise information to justify opinions and perspectives; Communicate using contextually appropriate Japanese. (Syllabus PDF p.3.)
- **Course structure:** four QCAA-developed units, each with a notional time of 55 hours of teaching and learning including assessment; students complete Units 1 and 2 before beginning Units 3 and 4, and Units 3 and 4 are studied as a pair. (Syllabus PDF p.5.)
- **Units:** Unit 1 私のくらし — My world (pp.10–11); Unit 2 私達の世界をたんけんする — Exploring our world (pp.12–13); Unit 3 私達の社会、文化とアイデンティティ — Our society; culture and identity (pp.14–15); Unit 4 私の現在と将来 — My present; my future (pp.16–17). Each unit has a subject-matter topic list that drives assessment alignment (e.g. IA1 relates to Unit 3 Topic 1 subject matter; IA2 to Unit 3 Topic 2 and/or Topic 3; IA3 and the external assessment to Unit 4 subject matter).
- **Assessment model:** Internal assessment 1 Examination — short response (20%); internal assessment 2 Examination — extended response (25%); internal assessment 3 Multimodal presentation and interview (30%); external assessment Examination — combination response (25%). (Syllabus PDF TOC and assessment sections pp.18–35.)
- **Support/assessment resources:** sample assessment instruments for IA1/IA2/IA3 (SRC-0005 to SRC-0007), QCAA confirmation submission information (SRC-0008), endorsement subject report 2026 (SRC-0009) and key senior subject changes for external assessment 2026 (SRC-0003).

## Representation & Hash Provenance

Per the coordinator's representation-hash ruling (`.superpowers/sdd/2026-08-30-australian-qld-teacher-skills-localisation/progress.md`), raw `%PDF-` bytes are preferred but not mandatory. Direct shell retrieval of the QCAA subject page and download endpoints returned Cloudflare HTTP 403 challenges in this environment this pass, and the Firecrawl retrieval service was unavailable (credits exhausted). The permitted documented-retrieval path was therefore used:

- **Retrieval method:** server-side render of each official QCAA URL via the `r.jina.ai` reader (a third-party reader that renders the authority URL server-side and returns a markdown/text rendering of the document body). The subject page (HTML) and each of the 11 linked PDFs returned a rendered body of the official document.
- **Representation type:** the rendered document body (markdown/text) of the official page/PDF, with the retrieval-service envelope (title / URL-source / published-time header and "Markdown Content:" framing) removed.
- **Hash input:** SHA-256 over the UTF-8 bytes of that cleaned rendered body only. It excludes the retrieval-service envelope, response headers and any challenge content.
- **Reproducibility limitation:** the hash is over the rendered body representation, not the raw PDF bytes. A follow-up validation that can fetch raw bytes should recompute hashes over the raw `%PDF-` stream for byte-exact provenance. The rendered body files used for hashing were generated under `/tmp/kilo/jp-repr/clean` on the retrieval date and are not committed; the hash values are recorded here and in the register. Cross-validation: the cleaned-body hashes for SRC-0003 and SRC-0008 (the two documents shared verbatim across language syllabuses) are byte-identical to the values recorded by the sibling Spanish package (QSUB-0072), confirming the hashing convention.
- **Permitted:** official-URL rendering; **prohibited and not used:** Wayback content, Cloudflare challenge HTML, metadata-only serialisations and fabricated hashes.
