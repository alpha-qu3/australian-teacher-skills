# Behaviour Scenarios for QCAA Food & Nutrition

## Lesson Plan Creation

**Scenario:** A teacher develops a 90-minute lesson plan for Year 11 Food & Nutrition students focusing on descriptive sensory profiling experimentation with vitamin- and mineral-rich foods (Unit 1, Topic 2). The lesson includes: introducing the lexicon concept for sensory profiling, conducting a rating/likeability test on various fruit and vegetable samples (fresh, frozen, dehydrated, canned), developing student lexicons for communication within tasting panels, and analysing results to explain preservation process effects on sensory properties. The lesson aligns with syllabus objectives 1 (recognise and describe), 2 (explain), 3 (analyse), 6 (generate solutions), and 7 (evaluate and refine) by having students create and evaluate sensory profiling methods for food quality assessment. Students work collaboratively to design experiments comparing sensory characteristics of different food preservation methods, then justify their recommendations for optimal preservation techniques based on sensory data collected.

## Lesson Differentiation

**Scenario:** A teacher differentiates a Unit 2, Topic 4 lesson on food formulations for consumer markets to support diverse learners including EAL/D students and students requiring additional processing time. For the core activity where students develop food formulations addressing consumer drivers (convenience, budget, perceived quality, ethics/culture), the teacher provides: simplified instruction sheets with visual flowcharts for EAL/D students; extended timelines and optional scaffolding worksheets for students needing additional processing time; advanced extension challenges for gifted students investigating innovative protein-based products (e.g., synthetic meat, ultra-high protein soybeans); and alternative assessment options allowing students to demonstrate understanding through oral presentations or visual diagrams instead of written reports. The differentiation maintains alignment with syllabus objectives 2 (explain ideas/problems), 5 (synthesise information/data), and 6 (generate solutions) while providing multiple means of engagement, representation, and expression.

## Check for Understanding

**Scenario:** During a Unit 3 lesson on carbohydrate-based food solutions, a teacher uses a strategic combination of exit tickets and practical demonstrations to check student understanding of the Analyse phase of the Food & Nutrition problem-solving process. Students are given a scenario involving a food product with texture and shelf-life issues related to carbohydrate processing and must: (1) identify constraints from stakeholder needs (success criteria), (2) analyse information and data to determine constituent parts and relationships, and (3) identify reasonableness of information related to the problem. Exit tickets prompt students to list two constraints they identified and explain how they analysed data to examine relationships. Practical demonstrations involve students explaining their analysis process while working with actual food samples (e.g., comparing fresh vs. dehydrated fruits). This checks understanding of syllabus objective 3 (analyse problems, information and data) specifically applied to carbohydrate-based food problems in Unit 3.

## Lesson Preparation

**Scenario:** A teacher prepares to introduce the food system concept (Unit 1, Topic 1) by reviewing syllabus requirements and gathering resources. Preparation includes: studying the food system diagram showing interconnected sectors (production, processing, distribution, consumption, research & development, sustainability, waste management, protection); identifying key principles affecting all sectors (sustainability, waste management, protection); preparing examples of macronutrients (protein, carbohydrate, fat, water, fibre) and micronutrients (fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K; water-soluble vitamins B1, B2, B3, C; minerals calcium, sodium, iron); and designing a hands-on activity where students map local food products to food system sectors. The teacher verifies that the lesson addresses syllabus objective 1 (recognise and describe facts and principles) by ensuring students can identify food system sectors and define principles affecting all sectors, while incorporating Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspectives on sustainable food practices as referenced in QCAA policies.
